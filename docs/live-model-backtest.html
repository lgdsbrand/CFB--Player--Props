Live model backtest

Phase L1: past games frozen every five minutes (plus three and one minute left), the live model's projection at each moment scored against the final. No odds anywhere: the prior is our own pregame projection, the state is the scoreboard.

How to read it. Brier and log loss score the home win %: lower is better, and 0.25 / 0.693 is a coin flip. Miss is the average absolute gap between the projected and the actual final margin or total, in points. 80% range held should read about 80%: higher means the ranges are too wide, lower too narrow. Every number is out of sample: fitted on earlier seasons, scored on the test season.
The methods.
  • Pregame only: the pregame projection held all game. The floor.
  • Score + clock, no prior: the same model with no team information, so every game starts even apart from the league's home edge.
  • A: the pregame projection fading with the clock, plus the score and the possession in progress (expected points from down and field position).
  • B: A plus how the game has played against the pregame view (points added per play so far, and points ahead of the projection).
  • nflverse win prob (NFL only): nflverse's own published win probability, their model without the betting line, read at the same snap. An outside benchmark.
What is left of a game is modelled as a Student t (fatter tails than a normal, fitted at each moment); the win % is then fitted to who actually won.

College football

Prior: the college game model's pregame projection, fitted on seasons before the test season. Recommended candidate on these numbers: A. B must beat A's log loss by 0.002 in every test season to be chosen, because it needs live play-by-play the free scoreboards do not carry.
